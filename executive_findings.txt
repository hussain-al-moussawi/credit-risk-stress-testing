
CREDIT DEFAULT PREDICTION & STRESS TESTING ENGINE
=================================================

EXECUTIVE FINDINGS

MODEL PERFORMANCE
-----------------
Random Forest ROC-AUC: 0.775
Logistic Regression ROC-AUC: 0.754

Random Forest F1 Score: 0.537
Logistic Regression F1 Score: 0.512

The Random Forest provided the stronger overall
discrimination and F1 performance, while Logistic
Regression achieved slightly higher recall.


PROBABILITY CALIBRATION
-----------------------
Observed test-set default rate:
22.13%

Calibrated average predicted default probability:
22.08%

Brier Score before calibration:
0.1696

Brier Score after calibration:
0.1362


RISK SEGMENTATION
-----------------
Very Low Risk observed default rate:
6.09%

Very High Risk observed default rate:
54.96%

The highest-risk 20% of customers captured
approximately 49.7% of all observed defaults.

The Very High Risk segment had approximately
2.48x the portfolio-wide default rate.


STRESS TEST
-----------
Base Case:
22.08%

Moderate Stress:
28.31%

Severe Stress:
47.17%

Relative increase under moderate stress:
28.2%

Relative increase under severe stress:
113.7%


HIGH-RISK CUSTOMER CHARACTERISTICS
----------------------------------
Compared with the full portfolio, the Very High Risk
segment displayed:

- substantially more delayed repayment months
- materially worse recent repayment behaviour
- approximately 60% lower average payments
- approximately 28% higher credit utilisation
- approximately 34% lower average credit limits


THRESHOLD ANALYSIS
------------------
Under an illustrative assumption that missing a real
default is five times as costly as falsely flagging a
non-defaulting borrower:

Standard threshold:
0.50

Cost-sensitive threshold:
0.15

Standard illustrative cost:
4378 units

Cost-sensitive illustrative cost:
3406 units

Recall increased from:
38.3%

to:
74.4%

This improvement came at the cost of substantially
more false-positive classifications.


LIMITATIONS
-----------
- Dataset represents Taiwanese credit-card customers.
- Stress scenarios are illustrative behavioural shocks,
  not regulatory or macroeconomic forecasts.
- Feature importance does not establish causality.
- Cost ratios used for threshold selection are
  illustrative rather than lender-specific.
- Calibration performance should not be interpreted
  as proof of perfect individual-level probability
  estimation.
